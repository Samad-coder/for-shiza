My Shiza,

Yesterday, I yelled at you, and I am truly sorry. You did not deserve to be spoken to that way—especially by the person who is supposed to be your partner and make you feel safe, respected, and loved.

I know that an apology cannot erase how I made you feel. I won't blame my emotions, the situation, or you for my choice to raise my voice. It was my responsibility to handle things differently, and I am sorry that I hurt you.

You deserve disagreement without disrespect, honesty without harshness, and love that feels safe even when things are difficult.

We are meant to begin a new chapter together on 15 November. That date makes me think even more seriously about the kind of husband and partner I want to be—not just on our wedding day, but in the ordinary moments that come after it.

I don't want to make big promises just because I am scared of losing you. I want to practise listening without interrupting, taking a pause when I feel overwhelmed, speaking calmly even when we disagree, and coming back to a conversation with care. I know change has to be shown consistently, not simply written in a letter.

I love you, and I am sorry for the pain I caused. I understand that you may need time, and you have every right to feel what you feel. You do not have to forgive me quickly or make me feel better about what happened.

I also hear that you don't want messages or a conversation from me right now. I will respect your space. This letter is not a request for an immediate reply, and our wedding date is not a reason for me to rush you.

If and when you feel ready, I want to listen to you properly—with patience, without defending myself, and without minimising your feelings. Until then, I will give you the room you asked for.

I'm truly sorry, Shiza. ♡
